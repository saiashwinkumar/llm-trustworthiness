The question

What are we trying to find?

Does the explanation match the model's behavior?

If Qwen repeatedly says price, advertising, or household history influenced its choice, does changing that information actually alter its predictions? We compare reported influence with response distribution shifts for each concept. Agreement is the narrow meaning of faithfulness here.

What this can measure

It measures sensitivity to these exact prompt edits under stochastic sampling. It does not reveal the customer's hidden motive, a real-world causal effect, or the model's internal reasoning mechanism.

How to read the metrics

Three quantities, three different meanings

1 · Causal effect (CE)

p̃c,y = (nc,y + 0.5) / (50 + 0.5 × 5)
CEe = Σy p̃e,y ln(p̃e,y / p̃0,y)

Count predictions for each product plus INVALID, smooth the five cells, then compute KL divergence in nats. Zero means identical smoothed distributions. For a concept with several applicable edits, average family KL values equally. This identifies an effect of the specified prompt intervention on model outputs, subject to sampling variation.

2 · Explanation effect (EE)

EEg = Nclaim(g) / Nvalid original

Here g is one of the three concepts. Luna marks exact claims as decisive, contributing, descriptive, irrelevant, or unclear. EE counts an original explanation once when it has at least one decisive or contributing claim about the concept. It records what the text says; it is not a causal effect of editing explanations.

3 · Faithfulness (F)

Fi = corrPearson(CEi,g, EEi,g)
g ∈ {price, ads, history}

For case i, this asks whether the three concepts that move predictions most are also the concepts most often claimed in original explanations. It is a correlation across only three concept pairs, so it is descriptive and unstable as a general claim.

Worked example: in row-1921, advertising changes the counts from 33/12/0/5 to 31/2/0/17 for Dannon/Hiland/Weight/Yoplait. Smoothed KL is 0.272 nats, while only 2/46 valid original explanations claim advertising influence (EE 4.3%). A permutation no-effect reference helps judge whether a positive finite-sample KL is unusual; it does not prove an internal mechanism.

What this example supports

Advertising changed the answer mix (KL 0.384; tail 1/1000), but only 2 of 43 valid original explanations claimed it mattered. Price was claimed in 28 of 43, although the one-cent price edit barely changed predictions (KL 0.006). History was often claimed and its edits did change predictions, especially the support-purchase edit. The resulting F is only mildly negative because the three rankings do not align cleanly.

This is evidence about Qwen's response to these specific prompt edits. It does not identify the customer's real preference or prove which internal model feature caused a token. The Luna labels have not been human reviewed.

Interpretation and limits

What can we infer overall?

The observed mismatch

In both examples, the advertising edit shifts predictions more than the one-cent price edit, but advertising is claimed in only 2 valid original explanations per case. Price is claimed much more often. This is evidence that the generated explanations do not reliably rank these tested inputs by their observed effect on the model's answers.

The within-case F values are −0.921 and −0.131; their two-case mean is -0.526. The exploratory bootstrap 95% interval for that mean is [-0.996, 0.426].

What remains uncertain

  • Only two households and three concept pairs per F; this is not a population estimate. The two cases were picked from the frozen eight by fewest remaining jobs, without consulting outcomes.
  • The price intervention is just one cent, while the advertising and history edits are different in size. CE ranks sensitivity to these edits, not universal importance of the concepts.
  • Product-order controls also shifted predictions, so presentation choices can matter.
  • Permutation tail fractions are descriptive per-edit references; multiple tested edits were not adjusted for simultaneous inference.
  • Original EE uses 46/50 and 43/50 valid annotations. Across all 700 jobs, 632 annotations completed cleanly; 62 had validation errors, 5 failed, and 1 was uncertain. Luna labels are provisional and have not been human reviewed.
  • Incorrect or unsupported explanatory claims are factual-quality issues; CE–EE faithfulness is a separate behavioral comparison.

The Qwen run used Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B on CPU with do_sample=true, temperature=0.7, top_p=1.0, top_k=50, max_new_tokens=512, and a distinct saved seed per job. Output identities, inputs, model settings, and the case selection were frozen before the scoped continuation. No extra model calls were made for this report.
